This mechanism does not use HTTP protocol, going beyond to use WebRTC protocol which comes by default in web browsers. Two browsers exchange a hashed code to establish a connection, talking directly. No communication happens to any server, making this a p2p secure channel, working even without internet if a LAN connection is made between the computers!

Sixteen thousand rooms gone!

By 1997, the country had roughly hundred PC bangs, which rised to fifteen thousand three years later, and by 2001 this peaked to 23,548 rooms, a room in nearly every commercial block of urban korea!

Contrarily, National Tax Services figures saw drop of 10568 rooms in 2017, down to 7,310 by 2024, approximately halving. Korea Creative Content Agency's white paper recorded 7243 for 2024, another recurring (fifth) consecutive fall, and by August 2025 this figure came down to 6,983. (which is roughly 70% reduction from peak)

This sounds like a drop, implying that the industry is failing. As a caution, KOCCA white papers and industry surveys slighly differ in reporting, which is why you may find some sources quoting "around 20k" for years when others report half of it. This trend is unambiguous because some PC Bangs count as business, others do not.

The rooms are closing. The business is growing.

Both sound contradictory, but the premise I am going to present might clarify. In 2024 PC bang industry revenue rose 12.8% to around approximately ₩2.26 trillion. This is a three times faster rise than Korean gaming industry on whole where fewer venues are paying more money!

But... why? Part of it is consolidation, those surviving this era of PC bang keep coming back to reinvest as whales. By rough estimates, 73% of operators did not require any PC upgrades at all in 2024 and only 6.3% of operators replaced their entire systems, hence making a PC bang environment only dependent on if the room and service is good or tired. Part of it is also that PC bang was never selling internet access to begin with, though it seems like, its popularity got widespread in gamers earlier on.

Around my birth, 94% of Korean households already had broadband and anyone walking into PC bang in 2006 had a connection home. They were not after internet, but buying the room to have friends in a row, shouting across and watching each other's screens. It acted like a digital environment where hangout was physical, being only service providing physical company over wires!

This is why demo above is floor plan rather than a simple chat window :) To show the empty rooms and tell what other people can achieve by filling the room only two people can right now.
