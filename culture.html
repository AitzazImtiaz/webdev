The Netflix Dilemna

By now, we established the earlier widespread of Korean network coverage; but as discussed a problem before, nobody had realize who pays for traffic costs when whole world starts watching. Scroll through for this case study!

A note on the line!

By May 2018 and September 2021, only two traffic measurements were made public across the dispute we will explore. Line between them is drawn dashed because of interpolation and not data. The dots can be considered as measurements.

January 2016

Netflix arrives

Foreign streaming arrives on Korean network after it spent a decade building capacity handling; Netflix became first company to become a user of it.

May 2018

50 Gbps

SK Broadband puts Netflix on its network at 50 GiB per seconds. It was remarkable at that time.

April 2020

Netflix sues first

Netflix files a case to Seoul court for confirmation that it has no obligation to pay network usage fees with the argument caucus of net neutrality: subscibers sufficiently pay for their broadband, and so the premise of charging content creator is double billing.

June 2021

Court disagrees

Seoul Central District Court provides ruling in favour for SK broadband, finding out in its verdict that it is reasonable for Netflix to provide something in return for service. Netflix provides a plea month later, warning that this ruling could topple global internet ecosystem.

September 2021

1,200 Gbps

Twenty four times the hike compared to 2018 figure, Squid Game first season launched the same month. SK Broadband files counterclaim seeking roughly 23 million dollars a year.

2022

Seven percent of everything

By first round of litigation, Netflix accounted for atleast 7% of South Korean internet traffic, as per figure from Korea Information Society Development Institute. The disputed sum for single year was 27.2 billion won.

September 2023

Both sides walk away mutually

Following five years, SK Broadband and Netflix dropped every claim and announced partnership, bundling Netflix with SK Telecom plans. SK Broadband however had the position that network usage fees still remain unchanged.

Why it mattered beyond Korea

This case was watched nationwide because it was first time a court told a content creator to pay an access network. Similar cases followed in Indonesia, Thailand, Brazil, the United States and the Carribean. Eight seperate bills because of this network usage fees reached Korean National Assembly.

This ended without an answer per se. With settlement withdrawl, claims got replaced with commerical parternship, which albeit is a resolution for two companies but not a general consensun. Everyone in legal sector watching for precedent got to see a deal instead.

Korean network made this collision sharp, three operators owning almost entire network cables, and so, a content provider negotiating access is negotiating an almost monopoly than a market. That structure was described as a premise in Network page and is the reason this argument surfaced when talking about the culture.
